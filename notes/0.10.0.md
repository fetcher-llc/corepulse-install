A new look, mute rules for chatty devices, and smaller improvements from daily use.

**A new look**

- The Control room design: a deep navy background with soft colour glows, glass panels, status tiles that
  glow in their state's colour, a see-through top bar, rounder corners and the Inter typeface. It is the
  dark theme ("Theme: Control room") and the default for anyone who has not picked a theme; light and the
  other themes get the same shapes.
- Summary page: a network health score ring next to the status strip, 24-hour trend lines in the Internet,
  Syslog, Firewall threats, Up and Down tiles, a "Live · updated N s ago" indicator, and numbers that count
  up when the page opens.
- Customize on the Summary page: hide any tile, panel or the health ring; hidden items wait in a Hidden tray
  to be shown again. Saved per user.

**Time zone**

- Settings → Time zone: pick the zone CorePulse shows every time in and runs its schedules by, with the
  current time there ticking on the page, and choose to follow daylight saving time or keep standard time
  all year. It takes effect everywhere within 30 seconds, no restart; until set, the zone chosen at
  installation (COREPULSE_TIMEZONE) applies.

**SNMP profiles**

- Edit a profile from the list (Settings → SNMP), including its name: the nodes using it keep it and get the
  new settings from their next poll. Pass phrases are never shown; leave them empty to keep the stored ones.

**Muted messages**

- Mute rules for messages a device keeps repeating: click Mute next to the event (or Settings → Muted
  messages), choose the device or any device, the text (numbers count as wildcards) and whether the event is
  still recorded without an alert or skipped altogether. Rules can end on a date and count how often they
  matched; the message itself always stays on the Syslog page.

**Application checks**

- Web page and Active Directory (LDAPS) checks have a new box, "Check the expiry date" (ticked by default, so
  nothing changes for existing checks). Untick it for a device whose expired certificate you accept: the
  date is then only shown in the result. "Verify the certificate" is now called "Require a trusted
  certificate", which says what it does.
- Web page checks explain the Expected status codes: the "?" lists what the common codes mean, and a line
  under the box says in words what the codes you typed accept (e.g. "success (2xx) and redirects (3xx)").

**Fixed**

- Alerts for syslog messages and traps could stay silent for up to 30 minutes after the CorePulse server
  started (the repeat guard measured time from the server's boot and treated "never alerted" as alerted).

**Updates**

- PostgreSQL 16 and Caddy are pinned to their newest builds (security fixes from upstream).
